In Today's Edition:

So, let’s start with collecting data from the EVM Nodes. Now there are two ways to go about this:

  1. Spin up your own Node (With Infura / quicknode)
  2. Build a centralized Database to collect data from a node.

Let’s rule out the possibilities that might not be an option for the scope of this newsletter.

In the first approach, we need to spin our own EVM node, which is possible if we go ahead with some of the offerings by Infura / Quicknode but in most cases, these solutions are expensive and therefore out of the scope of this discussion.

In the second approach, let’s talk in detail about how we could achieve this:

We would be using web3.py in order to extract data from the Node itself and then transform this data as per our needs.

Let’s try the said module to fetch the latest block:

from web3 import Web3
from decouple import config

node_url = config('Node_URL') # Configure Node_URL here
print(node_url)

# HTTPProvider:
w3 = Web3(Web3.HTTPProvider(node_url))
res = w3.isConnected()
print(res)

# Fetch Latest Block
latest_block = w3.eth.get_block('latest')

print(latest_block)

As you might have realized, the output from these queries quickly becomes complex and tedious when we talk about a larger-scale audience / multiple wallets.

In order to overcome those, we look into solutions where we store this extracted data in our own data warehouse as AWS RDS, Redshift, or for the folks on the GCP side, Google Cloud Relational Databases****.****

But this raises another issue, data warehouses are costly to maintain, and if we look into the effort-to-return ratio; it’s really not in favour of a data analyst who’s just looking to explore.

So for those of you, who are PRIMARILY interested in just analysing this data rather than engineering your own data infrastructure; here are a few possible ways to do so:

  1. 3rd party Data Infrastructure providers

The evaluation of 3rd Party Data infrastructure as offerings are beyond the scope of this edition, so I’ll go ahead and a mention a few that could get your journey started:

  1. Zettablock (free and paid editions)
  2. Dune (free and paid editions)
  3. FlipsideCrypto (API (need to mint your own key by Gas Fees) , Paid Enterprise data access)
  4. Luabase (Depreciated after being acquired by quicknode)

Zettablock:

ZettaBlock - The First Full-stack, Real-time Web3 Data Infra

Zettablock’s community edition has most of the data points you’d require to get started on your Data Analytics journey.

Some notable highlights:

  1. on-the-fly conversion of queries to GraphQL endpoints: If you have a query that you need to use on a website or mobile application you can convert it to a GraphQL endpoint from the console which opens up multiple doors of possibilities
  2. The community dashboards created by analysts are varied and accessible to get you started on your first queries.
  3. Support for dashboards would allow you to showcase your findings in the form of graphs, pie-charts which you could then share publicly to showcase your findings or even present as proof of work

Dune: https://dune.com/

Dune is one of the industry forerunners when it comes to community dashboards, I’m sure you’ve seen multiple posts from users talking about their findings through Dune Analytics.

Some notable highlights:

  1. There are a lot of community dashboards for you to get started with. Most of what you’d usually need, as NFT data (secondary sales volumes, trending collections, etc) or things such as ERC20 Token analytics and more
  2. You get access to most features in the free tier itself, the paid tier is meant for faster execution times or private queries.
  3. Here’s a sample Dune Dashboard I made - https://dune.com/gyanesh/everybody-is-a-ferrari-fan

These community offerings are some of the best ways to navigate data in the web3 space. Hope you took away some pointers on the bridge between data and web3!
