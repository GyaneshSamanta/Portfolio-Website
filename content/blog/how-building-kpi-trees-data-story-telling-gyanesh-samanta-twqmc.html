Newsletter Content

In my initial days into the product management field, I found it rather difficult to cconnect strategic objectives to measurable progress in a meaningful way. Eventually I came across KPI Trees, these are (arguably) one of the fastest ways to turn strategy into measurable outcomes.

KPI Tree Definition is a visualisation method that allows an organisation's goals to be broken down into more granular results. They enable us to visualise complex relationships, conflicts and interdependencies within an org. (We'll deep dive into this later on)

Key Benefits of KPI Trees:

  1. Explain the Approach: It'll probably take me around 2 hours to build the KPI trees used in this newsletter, but the beauty of KPI tree is, even a complex one should take you about 2-3 mins to completely understand.

  2. Simplying complex situations: We can naturally arrange target KPIs into meaningful groups, in a hierarchy. Generating summary gauges becomes relatively straightforward, all we need to decide on is the relative weights and arithmetic to blend the sub-measures.

  3. Foster Agreement: In a meeting with stakeholders from different teams, it is generally observable that there are certain dominant members in the group who have the ability to bounce a particular set of measures or data points through a mixture of raional argument (a strength of will). Developing KPI Trees avoids such situations as it puts a visble framework that anyone can easily challenge and question.

  4. Changes in Strategy: The roadmap of a business keeps on changing based on supply and demand in the market positioning of the business. Even if youj're lucky to hav a good roadmap, it will have to change -- possibly soon. Using KPI Tree approach means that you can see what impact changes in the roadmap will have on the measures.

Building a KPI Tree:

A KPI Tree structure has 4 levels of design, generally colour coded in this fashion:

  1. Strategic Level (Green Edges)

  2. Theme Level (Orange)

  3. Tactical Level (Purple)

  4. KPIs driving end results (Blue)

Here's an example I found on the internet for "being healthy" (Touching Grass):

Your Strategic objective here; 'be healthy' is similar to your company's NSM (North Star Metric), this is considered to be the 'goal' of the company and usually is responsible for driving the revenue of the organisation. In this case, 'be healthy' is the end strategic goal which we want to achieve, and to do so, the factors necessary are being plotted.

Definitions are boring, let's build one:

Step1: State your long Term Goals

Review existing missions, vision and senior approach documents and see if you can summarise your organisational priorities using a series of short, single-phrase sentences using general language.

Step2: Chunks - Break down your high level goals into smaller results and outcomes (must be measurable!)

This step is the most interesting to me, we need to identify outcomes that directly affect the box that we are 'chunking'. In the above image, being healthy is influenced by:

quality and regularity of sleep, diet, fitness.

Step3: Chat - Tuning the wording of the tree:

A KPI tree might use 5 words at maximum, including rich descriptive languages to describe the drivers. I'll link a useful resource for writing goal statements:

How to Write Goal Statements

Step4: Look out for common mistakes:

  • Using department names instead of effects and results (e.g. HR rather than 'We recruit appropriately skilled candidates')

  • Including targets and figures in the description. This can be highly emotive. Targets should only be set once a KPI has been agreed as being important (shortlisting)

  • Woolly words, which sound great but are vague and difficult to define, make assembling KPI Trees much harder. Instances of woolly words include bleeding-edge, outstanding and brilliant.

Step5: Connect - use connectors to show relationships and interactions:

Boxes on KPI tree should be linked to show a relation.

Two types of Links:

  1. Cause and Effect - Shows a positive coorelation, for example 'bad' sleep = 'bad' health

  2. Conflict - Shows a negative coorelation, for example 'less' unsaturated fats = 'good' health

Step6: Repeat 2-5 for next layer.

Step7: Identify KPIs for lowest level end results on tree:

By the time I get to the tactical level, it seems pretty straightforward to identify relevant KPIs. I've done this mistake earlier of trying to build up from bottonm to top in case of a KPI tree, but that often leads to errors, misses and problems (understandably, your NSM is driven by metrics not the other way round).

Generally a KPI tree shows all potential KPIs, both important and less important ones (defined by weight next to the box), with the help of these weights we can use a shortlisting method to identify and segregate just the important KPIs.
