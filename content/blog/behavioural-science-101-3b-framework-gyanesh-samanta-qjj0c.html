One of the key aspects of Product development is designing for Behaviour change.

A few questions every PM should ask themself when developing a product:

  1. How can I increase uptake of a new feature / program?

  2. Why are people dropping off halfway through X?

  3. How to increase the chances at engagement?

  4. The market specifies a need for this feature, yet they dont use the solution?

Behavioural science is a shortcut to building successful solutions. It unlocks the psychology behind why consumers act or don't act, this presents a roadmap on what to build and test. In this edition, we uncover the design principles of the 3B Framework in product management.

Chapter 1: Unearthing the Keystone Behavior

Step 1: Identify one KEY behaviour

  1. What do you want the user to do?

  2. Is this the most important behaviour to meet business goals?

  3. How often do you want the user to do this behaviour? (One time? Habitual?)

The most important step to change their behaviour is tracking the behaviour you seek to change.

Pitfalls:

  1. Not being specific enough. (Tip: Add recurrence interval)

  2. Mistaking an outcome as 'behaviour'

  3. Designated "log-in" as a behaviour to design for.

  4. Not aligning with stakeholders on agreed-on behaviour.

Question: What if I don't know the KEY behaviour?

  1. Can you get the data on how people behave today?

  2. Any Academic literature or research papers on the domain?

  3. A behaviour map outlining the steps you think customers are taking.

Test if your key behaviour drives the business Outcomes.

Chapter 2: Conquering the Barriers

To create the path of least resistence for any key behaviour for your consumer, remove or simplify as many decisions as possible. The path of least resistance in your product should lead to your desired key behaviour.

Barriers are points of Friction, they slow everything down

Cognitive Bias and Barriers:

Attention Bias:

  1. Do people remember it? (Availability Bias)

  2. Do people see it? (Saliency Bias)

  3. Do people want to see it? (Information Avoidance)

Cognitive Overload:

  1. Is the best option clear? (Choice Overload)

  2. Do people take lack time or energy? (Scarcity, Depletion)

  3. Do people lack confidence to decide now? (Procrastination, Decision Paralysis)

Status Quo:

  1. Do people Realise the opportunity cost of staying in the status Quo? (Opportunity Cost Neglect)

  2. Are there potential losses for moving away from the status quo? (Loss Aversion, Regret aversion, Sunk Costs)

  3. Is it easy for people to switch? (Cognitive overload, decision paralysis)

Mental Models:

  1. What are people's pre-existing attitude and beliefs?

  2. Do these beliefs align with the key behaviour? Are there assumptions different from what's being presented? (Presentation vs Perception)

Mistakes to Avoid:

  1. Forgetting that decision points are barriers.

  2. Dismissing all friction as bad friction.

Tackling Barriers:

Remove Entirely

  1. Pre-filled default values - based on say, most common historical responses.

  2. Avoiding open-ended questions: Forces user to come up with subjective answers in a form.

Simplify Decision

  1. Recommended Option / Tag.

  2. Share what most people are choosing "most popular" tag.

  3. Relatable description (Best Results observed at 3 times / week use)

  4. Instead of 5 possible options, present 3.

Chapter 3: Amplifying the Call of Benefits

Amplify Benefits:

  1. Benefits motivate user to overcome barriers.

  2. You need to make any existing benefits of your product salient and immediate, or even create new ones. Make sure they outweigh the costs / barriers. To drive long term benefits, focus on created a perceived immediate one from non-immediate ones.

Sometimes we need to reframe our product and give people the right choice for the wrong reason.

Example: Good dental hygiene in long term, but fresh breath on the daily! (Toothpaste & toothbrush combo set)

'Benefits' need not always be literal, like money or goods. Psycgological norms can also serve as motivational benefits.

Ask yourself:

  1. What would impact user's life right now?

  2. Does the benefit appeal to emotion?

  3. Is there a sense of urgency to act now?

  4. Do people receive a concrete benefit?

  5. Does the behaviour signal what is socially acceptable?

General Mistakes:

  1. Ignoring Psychological benefits.

  2. Overdoing it.
